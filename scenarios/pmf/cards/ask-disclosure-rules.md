# What must be in writing before a merchant agrees?

Paula Larkin accepted Kestrel's offer on Friday and the contract is out. I have her numbers ready. I do not have your rule, so I cannot say whether she is owed them in writing first.

## Known so far

- Paula's words on Friday were a decision: "We'll take Kestrel's." The contract went out by e-signature the same afternoon and has not come back.
- What is in writing to her: the amount and the term, in Marcus's mail on Thursday. What is not: the total she repays and the daily debit. Marcus's call may have covered both, and a call leaves nothing I can read.
- The numbers she is agreeing to, from Kestrel's own recorded offer: $80,000 advanced, $102,400 back over twelve months, $400 every working day. If your rule says they go in writing first, that note is drafted and waiting.
- Your firm has told me no rule. So the read that holds a close until the cost is on record has nothing to hold it against, and by its own rule it stays quiet rather than guess.

## What's missing

- The rule itself: what must be put in front of a merchant before they agree, in writing, in the places you sell. Commercial financing disclosure is law in some states and not others, and which words it takes is yours to say, never mine to invent.
- Whether a call counts. If Marcus reading the numbers out on Thursday satisfies it, say so, and I will stop counting only what was written.

## What your answer changes

- I hold every close against your list and say which of it is not yet in writing on the thread, starting with Paula's today.
- If you tell me nothing is required, I stop raising it. The numbers stay ready for the moments you want them sent anyway.
- I will never say a disclosure was made off a call nobody captured, and never say one was owed off a rule nobody told me.

## Where your answer is saved

- As a rule on your organisation, marked compliance, hard or soft as you say. Dated, editable, and checked against every close from then on.

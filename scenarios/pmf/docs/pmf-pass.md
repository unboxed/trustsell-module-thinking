# The broker's world, worked from both ends

Job 3 of `NEXT.md`, run on the world the library was not derived from: a merchant cash advance
broker, twelve cards, a tempo of hours, measured against the volume seller's twenty-two questions
(`V`). The same six steps as the council world, per person, and the record grows a section at a
time as each group of people is worked. Up from the records in `world/records.md`, through the
counts, to what fires; down from the seller's questions; the cards opened last. The question held
throughout is the one `NEXT.md` sets: when a read that fires at the patient end cannot apply here,
does the question it answered there have any other answer in this world?

### 1. The records

Written in `world/records.md` as fiction, keeping every fact the cards implied where the facts
could all be true, and settling where they could not.

- **The clock.** Every row carries a time. The tool's day begins at 08:00 on Monday 21
  September, and the last of Tony's four answers landed at 08:20. "Since Friday" on the cast's
  line and "Monday" on the card's timeline could not both be true of all four answers; the records
  put Northline's decline and Avondale's two offers on Friday, the last two after you had stopped
  for the day, and Bayard's this morning. The card says so now.
- **Text is the conversation.** Each merchant has one form, one text conversation of a handful
  of messages, and an upload. The only mail with a merchant is Marcus's thread with Paula, because
  the offer went by mail. Nothing is by phone that the tool can read, and three calls that matter
  (yours with Paula on the 7th, Marcus's with her on the 17th, and whatever was said to Tony) are
  on no record.
- **Alma sent a year, not three months.** The card said her statements "cover enough periods to
  show the swing", and three summer statements cannot show a winter. She sent twelve months from
  her banking app because it was easier than picking three, which is the kind of thing that
  happens, and the twelve are in the section month by month.
- **Paula's cost is half in writing.** Marcus's mail of Thursday names the amount and the term
  and not the total repayable or the daily debit; his call may have said both. That is the fact
  the card turns on, and it is written down rather than assumed either way.
- **Renee's file went short on Thursday evening**, in Marcus's own note on the CRM, and nobody
  has told her. The request named June, July and August, so the one still out can be named.
- **The processor is on record only where he writes.** Marcus's activity lines and his one mail
  thread are what the tool holds of him; his calls are not. Hannah Beck is a funder's person, and
  nothing counts a funder as a person: her answers are counted against the deals they land on.

Five things the counts said about themselves while being run:

- **`required-disclosures` at zero silences the read, and the card had not noticed.** The read's

  rather than lenient". The card said the rules were unwritten in its *Still unclear* and rested on

  twenty-second pass's lesson arriving at the fast end.
- **`since-an-answer-landed` counts working time and the card needs hours.** The count says "the
  working time between the day the newest answer was recorded and now"; on a morning where the
  newest answer is forty minutes old, the honest figure is the clock, and the section gives it.
  The count's words say "day"; the arithmetic does not care.
- **Three statements cannot show a season.** `money-in-swing-vs-own-periods` says its own floor is
  about six periods, and the trade asks for three. A read about seasonal income at this end
  depends on a merchant sending more than was asked for, or on a bank connection the trade wishes
  for and nobody offers. The card stands on a year because the fiction gave it one.
- **A pattern the count sees and cannot name.** $612 to "Ford Credit" on the 3rd of every month
  is `repeated-outgoings-to-one-payee`'s whole job, and without the told names in the field it is
  a payee and nothing more. That is `ask-known-lenders`' case, made on a real row.
- **A call leaves nothing, three times in four people.** What was said to Paula on two calls

  reads say so, and at this tempo it is most of what happens.

# Who do you know at the twelve councils without a name?

Twelve of your thirty have nobody named. Before I go looking for people on their websites, I'd rather know who you already know. That is on no record I can read.

## Known so far

- What the twelve have published: four have a digital strategy out this year, Glenfield, Kirby, Linton and Orton, and only Glenfield's mentions case management. Ashby has a tender out for "customer contact modernisation". Two have new digital leads since spring, Kirby and Carlton.
- Nothing on record says who you or Simon know at any of them: not a thread, not a contact, not a line in Slack. Every one of the twelve is cold to you as far as I can see.
- If you know nobody, I'd start with Ashby, Glenfield and Kirby: a live tender, a strategy that names the thing, and a new lead. That order is mine, on a fit you have told me in a sentence.

## What's missing

- Who you know. A name from you beats a name from a website, and it changes the order more than anything published does.
- What a good fit looks like, beyond "a council". Until you tell me (the other card asks), I am ranking on what the web shows and not on your reason.

## What your answer changes

- For each you tick: I start with the person you name, and how you know them is written down so a first note can say so.
- For the rest: I rank them by what they have published and go looking for the likely lead, three at a time. The others wait until those three have names.

## Where your answer is saved

- On your list of thirty, marked "you know someone here", with the date, and a note against each person you name.

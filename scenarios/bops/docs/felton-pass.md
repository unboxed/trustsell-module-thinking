# Felton, worked from both ends

The sixth, seventh and eighth people run through the method in `NEXT.md`, Job 1, on 22 September
2026: Stephen Walsh, Shabana Akhtar and David Marshall, who share four threads and one demo. Up
from the records in `world/records.md`, through the counts, to what fires; down from the seller's
questions; the two cards opened last. The method is the one agreed on Karen Hughes
(`karen-hughes-pass.md`). This is the first run on a price objection, the first test of the
Offerings reads from the records up, and the first world with a captured call.

## 1. The records

Written in `world/records.md` as fiction, keeping every fact the two cards implied where the facts
could all be true, and settling the places where they could not.

- **Eighteen messages since June, Stephen's four all about price.** Held as written. Four threads,
  because the director card said "every Felton thread" and the price card said "the Felton
  thread": eighteen messages on four threads, seven hers, seven yours, four his.
- **"Last Wednesday."** The card arrives on Friday 18 September and quoted "last Wednesday", which
  from a Friday is either the 9th or the 16th. The record puts his message on Wednesday 9
  September and the card now says the date.
- **Each of his messages shorter than the last.** The card said only that all four mention the
  price. `blocker` needs the replies to have gone short against his own rhythm in the thread, so
  the records give him ninety words, then sixty, forty-five, thirty. That is the world absorbing
  what the read needs and the card did not say; if the user would rather he wrote at one length
  throughout, the read does not fire and the card is a different card.
- **Two next steps that stalled.** Nothing in either card. The read needs them, and they are the
  natural shape of the history the cards implied: you offered a call in July and Shabana said
  she would set it up; you proposed a walk-through in August and Stephen answered with the worry.
- **The demo, recorded.** Every other captured call in this world was held on Meet and not
  recorded. Felton's was, so that `advocacy-voiced-on-call` and `objection-raised-on-call` are
  run on something for the first time. Two team leaders were at it, now in `cast.md`, because
  `champion` needs colleagues brought in and the card claimed she was for it.
- **Her promise.** The director card grades "I'll need David onside" as loose words, correctly.
  The records add the concrete promise the cards never mentioned: "I'll set something up with
  Stephen for early August", 24 July, and nothing shows it happening. Stephen wrote instead.
- **The partial rollout price sheet against the price list** (`world/offering.md`, flagged). The
  profile says the price list "replaces all per-team and per-month pricing" and prices nothing
  smaller than a licence per authority. Settled as fiction: the sheet is your own one-page quote,
  written on 6 July after Bramley asked in June whether they could start with one service, and
  the profile has never been told about it. So `your-price` gives £45,000 a year and nothing
  smaller, and the card's "about a third" rests on the document in Drive, not on a count.
- **Felton's size.** Nothing in the world said which band Felton falls in. The web now holds a
  district of about 110,000 people, so the £45,000 the cards implied is the profile's own arithmetic.

Four things the counts said about themselves while being run:

- **`people-covered-vs-needed` says the roles are told and reaches no told record that holds
  them.** Its body says the cast a decision needs comes "from what you told the tool". Its
  `needs` are mail, calendar, contacts and LinkedIn, and the map it is done over gathers the
  seed contact and the relationship note and nothing from the offering. In this world the roles
  are in the profile's Customer field, which is `offering-told#fit-shape`, and the build refused
  the card's source row that said so. The card rests the roles on the goal instead, which does
  say several people are needed, but not which. Either the map gathers the fit-shape, or the
  count is not told what it says it is.
- **The count cannot place the person who holds the objection.** Stephen is finance, the
  profile's cast has no finance role, and the count's own "What it cannot see" names exactly
  this: a role that exists at this organisation and not on your list. The person the deal is
  stuck on is a row the map has no slot for.
- **`repeated-objection-count` cannot tell one worry from two.** Its body says so. Stephen's
  first two messages are about the size of the number and his last two about this year's
  budget. The partial rollout answers the first and only half answers the second, and the card
  now says so in *Still unclear*, because the count cannot.
- **`your-price` has no place for a smaller first step.** The Pricing told record is price,
  discount room, term and how it is paid. The partial rollout is not discount room, because it is
  a different scope at its own price, and it is not a term. "One entry is one thing on one set of
  terms" would make it a second entry on the offering list, and then `where-this-one-goes` would
  have something to choose between in the world that ships. Nobody has decided that, so the
  sheet stays a document in Drive that a card can point at and no count can quote.

## 5. Where the two ends meet

Opened last. Four verdicts from `NEXT.md`.

**`act-felton-price`**

- blocker, Q15 and Q16: **both ends reach it.** The card should exist, and it is right about
  the move. `likely` holds and now says why: four messages, each shorter.
- price-barrier: **the wrong join.** The read compares your price with named rivals' published
  prices and your discount room, and this world has none of the three. Stephen never compared
  you with anybody; he compared you with this year's budget. Came off, and `your-price` stayed
  through the read that actually uses it here.
- can-they-carry-it: **reached through no read the card named.** The card's move is a smaller
  number, and the read that says the number is too big for them is this one, from the volume
  seller's set, softly, on his words. Now supporting. The first time a read written for a broker
  fires at a council.
- answer-gap: **reached through no read the card named.** The worry has no typed answer and one
  document on file, and the card sends that document. It is also the only read on the card that
  reaches Drive, which the build required before the card could point at the sheet.
- The message: **no sanctioned opening.** The rows have four openers: what is owed, a fresh
  reason, their date, the angle. Nothing was owed to Stephen, nothing was published, and the
  angle was not the point. The message opens on his own sentence. A worry in their own words is
  now a fifth opener, the two counts that keep the passages, in `build.js` and in
  `message-shape.md`. *(My reason, not yet yours.)*
- The figures: 18 messages held once the world had them; "last Wednesday" became the date.
- The draft said "the rest is there when the next budget round allows". Now "at the same rate",
  because the sheet says so and the card's *holds back* forbids the word discount.

**`act-felton-director`**

  carry their message rows.
- `blocker` and `can-they-carry-it` carry `assumes_status: decided`: the first could only have

  `message-shape.md`.
- The price sheet against the price list is settled in `world/offering.md` as a quote the profile
  was never told about.

Findings for the library, recorded in `coverage.md` and not fixed here:

- `people-covered-vs-needed` says the roles are told and gathers no told record that holds them.
- The stakeholder map has no slot for a role not on the told list, and at Felton that is the
  person the deal is stuck on.
- `repeated-objection-count` cannot tell one worry from two, and the one worry at Felton is two.
- The Pricing told record has no place for a smaller first step, and the alternative, a second
  entry on the offering list, is undecided.
- `price-barrier` has nothing to read in a market whose competitor is "do nothing". That is not a
  fault; it is the read declaring what it needs. But Q15 at this council is about affordability,
  and the read that answered it came from the other seller's set.
- A card's `arrives` is the world's fiction, not the read's, again: blocker fired on 9 September
  and the card is shown on the 18th.

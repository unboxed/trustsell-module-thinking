# Holbrook, worked from both ends

The fourth person run through the method in `NEXT.md`, Job 1, on 22 September 2026: Neil
Chapman, one person with two cards that arrived on the same day. Up from the records in
`world/records.md`, through the counts, to what fires; down from the seller's questions; the two
cards opened last. The method is the one agreed on Karen Hughes (`karen-hughes-pass.md`) and run
on Westmoor (`westmoor-pass.md`).

## 1. The records

Written in `world/records.md` as fiction, keeping every fact the two cards implied where the
facts could all be true, and settling the places where they could not.

- **"Six weeks since you last spoke."** True of the calls: the last was 3 August. But the
  reconnect card's own Sources named his email of 19 August, and the count it rests on measures
  how long since *anything* passed. From your reply of 20 August it is eighteen working days,
  about four weeks. The records keep both facts; the six weeks is the gap between calls, and the
  card now says so.
- **"Every five to seven weeks."** Four calls give three gaps: seven weeks, six, and seven. The
  records say six or seven.
- **Eleven messages and four calls** held as written. Five threads, one opened by him, which is
  the thread that matters.
- **What he asked and what you said.** The reconnect card said he asked in August how East
  Marston "did it in stages" and that there is "now a note on it". Nothing said what you replied.
  The records give you a reply the next day, "let me write up how East Marston did it and send it
  over", because a seller who lets a champion's question sit nineteen working days without a word
  is a different seller from the one the world describes. That reply turns his question into your
  promise, and the card into a debt card.
- **The window, in his words, with a reason.** The card quoted "the window for this year closes
  16 October". The records add the sentence after it, "anything not in by then is next year's
  money", because `deadline-they-gave` grades high only when a reason came with the date.

Two things the counts said about themselves while being run:

- **`time-to-reconnect` failed both halves at once.** He is not quieter than his usual, and
  there is nothing fresh on the web. The card had read the gap between *calls* as the quiet, and a
  file in your own Drive as the fresh reason; the count that measures the quiet counts messages
  too, and the count that finds the reason looks only at what is published. Neither half was ever
  going to hold on this world, and the card was right about the move anyway, because the move was

## 5. Where the two ends meet

Opened last. Four verdicts from `NEXT.md`.

**`act-neil-rhythm`** (the id kept, so that nothing pointing at it breaks; the card is now the
phasing note)

- time-to-reconnect: **the wrong join, on both halves.** Came off.
- loose-end, Q22 and F9: **reached through no read the card named.** His question and your
  promise are the whole card. Now the main read, and `sure` rather than `likely`: it stands on
  his words and yours, not on a pattern.
- best-way-to-reach: **both ends reach it**, thinly: it carries the one figure the card needs
  about how he takes a meeting.
- "Six weeks", "every five to seven weeks", "answers within two days": **figures the world could
  not produce as written.** The first was the gap between calls; the second was three gaps of
  seven, six and seven; the third holds, on exactly four replies. All rewritten.
- `arrives`: **moved from the 18th to the 16th.** A promise eighteen working days old is owed
  today, not on Friday; the world's week starts on the 16th and nothing on the card justified
  waiting two days.
- The draft now says you promised it and that it took too long, and asks nothing, which is the
  shape `pushing` allows and story ch. 8 asks for: the gift first, no nudge.

**`act-holbrook-window`**

- buying-intent: **the wrong main read.** It softens to "showing interest" on one money cue and
  one timing question, and the card's `sure: "sure"` was never about intent. It was about the
  date. Now supporting.
- deadline-they-gave, F10: **both ends reach it.** The date is his, with a reason, and the time
  left has crossed into short for a patient sale. Now the main read, and `sure` holds under it.
- time-to-reconnect: **the wrong join**, same reason as above. Came off.
- best-way-to-reach: **both ends reach it**, for "he has never proposed a meeting".
- "Twenty-two working days" and "send that first; propose the meeting in the same breath or two
  days later": **the card and its front matter disagreed.** Both cards arrived on the 18th, so the
  card's own spacing could not exist. Now the note arrives on the 16th and the invite on the
  18th, and the build holds the invite behind the note, which is what the card said it wanted.
- The two cards were **not merged.** Argued: the note pays a debt and should ask nothing (Q19,
  "not as a chase"); the invite is a move and rests on his own date. On a patient sale two days
  between the gift and the ask cost nothing against twenty-two working days, and the day's rule,
  what gives before what asks, is the same rule at a smaller scale. The alternative, one message
  with the note and the ask, is what the fast end would do and is recorded here for Job 3.

**Supply with no demand**: none. Every read that fires answers a question the seller asks.

**Demand with no supply**: Q20 again. Job 2.

Findings for the library, recorded in `coverage.md` and not fixed here:

- `time-to-reconnect` needs the web, and in this world the web has nothing to say about any
  council. Three cards rest on it in `bops` (Neil's, and two of Lisa Holmes's); Neil's is now off
  it, and Lisa's are the next test, because hers claim a real published reason.
- `time-to-their-date` is a figure true on a day, and a card shows on a day that is not the
  world's today. The figure table carries both; nothing in the build knows a card's figures are
  dated.
- A promise with no date given ("let me write it up and send it over") is concrete in its object
  and loose in its time. `promise-made-undelivered` counts it and `loose-end` softens on it; the
  two agree, and the card says `sure` on the question rather than on the promise.

# Tarlton, worked from both ends

The twelfth person run through the method in `NEXT.md`, Job 1, on 22 September 2026: Darren
Cole, the procurement contact named on Tarlton Borough Council's tender, and the first person in
this world you have never exchanged anything with and whose organisation has never exchanged
anything with you. Two cards: the clarification questions, arriving on 18 September, and the Ask
about bidding, held behind it until the questions are in. Up from the records in
`world/records.md`, through the counts, to what fires; down from the seller's questions; the cards
opened last. The method is the one agreed on Karen Hughes (`karen-hughes-pass.md`). This is the
first run of `fit-match` and `stated-priorities` from the records up, and the first card whose
every record was published by the buyer rather than passed between you.

## 1. The records

Written in `world/records.md` as fiction, keeping every fact the two cards implied. Nothing on
them contradicted anything else, because there was almost nothing: the cards quoted a notice, a
PDF and your list. What the section adds is what a tender notice would actually carry, and what
the rest of the channels hold, which is nothing.

- **Everything is on the web, and all of it is theirs.** The notice on Tarlton's portal,
  published Wednesday 9 September: the title, the close on 9 October, clarifications by 2 October
  through the portal's messaging, Darren Cole as the contact, and the rule that bidders must not
  approach officers directly. The PDF behind it: section 2.3, "reduce officer time on case
  administration"; section 4, "work alongside existing systems"; section 3, the scale as about
  40,000 cases a year across three services, and no count of officers. No estimated value. The
  council's own page gives its population as about 120,000.
- **Nothing anywhere else.** No thread with anyone at tarlton.gov.uk, nothing in Calendar, Meet
  or Contacts, nothing shared from Drive, nobody from Tarlton in Slack and no mention of it there.
  Simon Ward has never named the council. LinkedIn is not connected.
- **The shelf.** Three things on it speak to the tender's two sentences: the officer-time
  one-pager, the alongside one-pager and the East Marston case study. None has been sent, and the
  rules mean none can be.
- **"Last Wednesday" is 9 September.** The card, arriving Friday 18 September, said the tender
  went out "last Wednesday", which from a Friday reads as the 16th. It says 9 September now.

Four things the counts said about themselves while being run:

- **A date the buyer published is counted by nothing.** `dated-facts-in-their-words` is the only
  count of the buyer's own dates, and it reads threads: a date has to have been said to you. The
  clarification close and the tender close are the two most consequential dates in this world,
  they are in Tarlton's own words, and no count holds them, so `time-to-their-date` has nothing
  to count down to and `deadline-they-gave` cannot fire. The card's *when* rests on the web record
  directly, the way North Ridley's rested on the calendar invite. Reading principle 6 says a date
  they put in writing is a fact on record; the count under the deadline read hears only what was
  written to you. The firm world's tender card did not meet this because there the buyer said the
  date on a thread. The same is true of the notice's rule of contact: Q9, the best way to reach
  them, is answered by the notice and read by no count.
- **Procurement on the web is not procurement to `budget-or-procurement-entered`.** The card
  quoted that count, through `buying-intent`, for "a tender is procurement". The count reads a
  conversation, and there is none. A tender at a cold organisation is the plainest buying signal
  there is and it reaches `buying-intent` through none of its five counts.
- **The fit's unknown column is what the clarification questions ask.** `fit-factors-met` keeps
  unknown apart from missed on purpose. The two questions in the draft are the two things the
  fit could not settle from the document: whether a baseline for officer time exists, and whether
  replacing the current system is in scope. That is the count doing the message's work without
  anything wiring it: nothing in the library says a clarification question is an unknown asked
  as a fact, and this card shows it is.
- **Cold is told here, not counted.** `target-cold-to-you` sits under `warm-path` and
  `customer-introduction`, and neither fires: no bridge, no customer's name. So the card's "you
  have never spoken to anyone there" rests on your list, where Tarlton stands at cold, and not on
  the mailbox, which would confirm it through a count nothing on the card can reach. On a cold
  organisation the ladder and the mailbox agree; where a seller's list is stale they would not,
  and no read on this card would notice.

## 5. Where the two ends meet

Opened last. Four verdicts from `NEXT.md`.

**`act-tarlton-tender`**

- fit-match, Q3 and Q20: **both ends reach it.** The read fires on their words, and the two
  questions in the draft are its unknown column. The card is right about the move.
- stated-priorities: **reached through no read the card named.** "Those are your two sentences"
  was the card quoting the buyer's own passages, with their date, and that is this read's whole
  job. Now supporting, and the message opens on it.
- buying-intent: **both ends reach it through the wrong join.** The card was right that a tender
  is a buying signal and wrong about the count that says so: `budget-or-procurement-entered` is 0,
  because it reads a conversation and there is none. Came off, with its count.
- warm-path: **kept for its gate.** A supporting read that does not fire, held so the card could
  quote `target-cold-to-you`, which is the Karen case again. Came off, with its count; "cold" now
  rests on your list.
- The date: **reached through no count at all.** The card's *when* and its sure word rest on the
  notice directly. Recorded above and in `coverage.md`.
- The message rows: the opening rests on the passages, which is a sanctioned opener; it points at
  the tender's own sections and nothing of yours; it asks two facts, not one; and it holds back
  everything about BOPS, because a clarification's answer is published to every bidder. The
  "at most one ask" rule in `message-shape.md` was written for a note to a person's attention. A
  portal's Q&A takes questions one at a time and answers each in public, so two questions are two
  entries, not two asks in one note. *(My reason, not yet yours.)*

**`ask-tarlton-bid`**

- fit-match, F19: **demand with no supply, and correctly so.** The read carries what is known,
  two of three met and nothing missed, and the question the card asks is one the map already
  says no read answers: whether the effort is worth spending is the Brain's, and nothing holds the
  goal's economics. A told gap, so an Ask. Decided on fit-match for what it knows, with nothing
  supporting, because nothing else fires.
- **Where the answer is saved had no record.** The card said "on Tarlton's page, as your intent".
  The record it becomes is a deal seed, `people-told#deal-seed`: a deal you name, on Tarlton, for
  this tender, the record every gather about a live sale starts from. Until it exists Tarlton has
  a fit and no deal, which is why no deal read can be made there. The card says so now, and
  "We'll bid" names the deal before it gathers anything.
- The Ask asks for a fact about the seller, their intent, not a verdict on the evidence; the
  template's rule holds.

**Supply with no demand**: none. Both reads that fire are carried.

**Demand with no supply**: Q9, the rule of contact, answered by the notice and read by nothing;
Q8, answered by absence with LinkedIn held back; F19, by the Ask.

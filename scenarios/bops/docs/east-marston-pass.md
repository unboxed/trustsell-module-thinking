you are not selling to, and no card is about him or to him. Eight of the twenty-five cards quote

Five things the counts said about themselves while being run:

- **The offer reached no count.** The Westmoor pass found it and named it; run from the customer's
  end it is the whole of what his records say. `customer-introduction` counts names a customer
  gives you, the other direction; the shelf's counts read Drive and a told tag. A customer who
  will speak was evidence with nowhere to be counted. `offers-to-vouch` exists now, over the deal
  since the sale, and `answer-gap` reads it beside `proof-on-file`: a worry only a peer can carry
  has an answer on file when a customer has offered, graded like a case study, third-party by who
  speaks and aged from the day it was made. *(My reason, not yet yours.)*
- **An offer is not a booking, and the count keeps the date so the read can say so.** Six months
  unasked is an offer to check, not a call to promise. The card that quoted the offer was
  promising it ("I'll set it up"). The count's `breaks` is this sentence, and it is what changed
  the draft.
- **The shelf has no tags, so `proof-on-file` cannot say what anything proves.** Three documents
  about one customer, and nothing told says which worry each answers; every card that sends one
  makes the match by hand and says so. The thirty-fourth pass found it from the offering's side;
  this is the same hole seen from the evidence's.
- **`third-party-vs-self-asserted` cannot hold a customer's sign-off.** The case study is yours
  by who wrote it, and the customer read the draft and corrected two quotes. The count sorts by
  producer and says its commonest flattery is the paid third party; the opposite case, your own
  document checked by the customer it names, it cannot see either.
- **How recently a deal completed stands on nothing told.** No deal outcome is typed, the CRM is
  not connected, and the rung on your list says where East Marston stands but not when it got
  there. "Fourteen months" is in the goal's prose and the profile's, and the count reports it from
  there and says so.

And one thing the counts cannot do at all: say that the same request came from five people at
four councils. `their-question-unanswered` counts per person; `worry-occurrences-across-deals`,
under `recurring-objection`, counts a worry across deals, and whether "does it work at a real
council" is a worry or a request for proof is a reading nobody has made.

## 5. Where the two ends meet

Opened last. Four verdicts from `NEXT.md`, on the one card that touches him and on the card that
does not exist.

**`act-rachel-peer-call`**, re-tested from the customer's end

- answer-gap, Q14 and Q17: **both ends reach it**, and they reached it through a count that did
  not exist. The card's strongest fact, the offer, was a Sources row resting on Gmail directly,
  which the build accepts and no read produced. It rests on `offers-to-vouch` now, in *points at*
  and in *holds back*, and Andrew Doyle is among the card's `about`, so the figure rule reads his
  section for the offer's date and age.
- **A promise the seller could not make.** The draft said "I'll set it up". The offer is six
  months old, has never been used, and nobody has asked him since whether it stands; a call
  promised to a buyer on that is a call the seller may not be able to deliver, and the customer is
  the one person in the world you cannot afford to surprise. The draft says "I'll ask him" now,
  the card's *holds back* names the promise, and the watch's next step is the note to Andrew
  rather than a briefing "on what he is likely to say", which the tool has nothing to base on.
  *(My reason, not yet yours.)*
- Everything else on the card held: the sure word, the opening on her question asked twice, the
  page as the quieter path.

**The card to Andrew, which does not exist**

- **Neither end reaches it today.** Nothing fires on him: nothing is owed, nothing is open, he has
  named nobody. And none of the sixty-nine questions asks anything about a customer except what
  they could introduce you to. So the library has nothing to say to the customer whose name is in
  eight cards, and that is the honest result rather than a fault: the tool covers from a lead
  onwards and the sale ends the ladder.
- **The first card to a customer is the one the watch turns up.** If Rachel says yes, the next
  move is a note to Andrew that opens on his own offer, asks whether it stands, and names the
  council. That is a card a watch produces, and it will be the first in any world written to a
  customer. Not written today, because today he is not asked anything.
- What is worth noticing: a customer read your case study in draft and offered his time in March,
  and in the six months since the tool would have suggested nothing to him, while five buyers
  asked for exactly what he offered. A seller would say that is a relationship going unwatered. No
  question in the three sets asks it and no read says it. Named, not built.

**Supply with no demand**: `delivered-before` fires softly on every cold council in this world and
no card carries it. Argued: with one customer, the case study says the same thing with a figure
in it, and every card that could lead with "you have done this before" sends the case study
instead. The read earns its place where there are several sales and no write-up, which is the
other end of the spectrum. Right that no card carries it here.

**Demand with no supply**: F13's second half, what it saved them, is on the shelf in a document
the seller wrote, and `delivered-before` is right to keep that apart from the plain fact of the
sale. And Q12 across the shelf: nothing told says which of three documents answers which worry,
which is the missing proof tag, found for the third time.

Findings for the library, recorded in `coverage.md` and not fixed here:

- The tool never writes to a customer. Nothing fires on a person past the sale in a patient world,
  and no question asks about one, so a customer whose name carries eight cards is somebody the
  tool would never suggest a word to. The first such card is the one the peer-call watch turns up.
- The same request for a live council's word, from five people at four councils, is counted by
  nothing across deals. Whether it is a worry (`worry-occurrences-across-deals`) or a request for
  proof is a reading nobody has made.
- `third-party-vs-self-asserted` cannot hold a customer's sign-off on your own document; it sorts
  by who wrote it.
- The rung at which a sale completes was named by no world until today, and `world/goal.md`
  carries it in prose, not in a field the build reads.
- The shelf has no proof tags in this world, seen now from the evidence's side: three documents
  about one customer, and every match to a worry made by hand.

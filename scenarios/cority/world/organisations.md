## What makes them one kind of buyer

All six are large industrial groups with a safety function, a works council or its equivalent, and
an IT department with a digitisation plan. That is the told fit-shape, and it is the key the
process below hangs on: a buy at any of them takes the same shape, whatever they make.

## What a buy here takes on their side

You have told the tool this, as `people-told#buying-process` against the fit-shape above. It is the
told record written in the twenty-second pass, and this is the first world that fills it.

| Step | What it looks like when it has happened | Roughly when | Can you help |
|---|---|---|---|
| A case for the spend | A number somebody inside has put their name to, usually built from their own published figures | Early, before anything formal | Yes. You build the first version from their annual report |
| An IT discussion | Somebody from IT on a thread or in a meeting, asking about integration or security | Middle, and it decides how long the rest takes | Yes. The standing security answers, and a call with a solutions engineer |
| Budget approved | A number named to you, or a procurement process opening | Middle to late | No. This one is theirs |
| A works council or union consultation | Mentioned by name, usually as a reason for a delay | Late, and routinely forgotten until it bites | Partly. What other groups did, in a note |
| Signature | The contract back | Last | No |

The middle three are the ones the record can be silent about for months, which is what
[`steps-on-their-side`](../../../signals/steps-on-their-side.md) is built to say out loud without
claiming they have not happened.

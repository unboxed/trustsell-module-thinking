# The firm's world, worked from both ends

Job 3 of `NEXT.md`, run on the second world the library was not derived from: an enterprise seller
with a firm around her, seven cards, a patient tempo and a formal process on the buyer's side,
measured against the firm seller's twenty-one questions (`F`). The same six steps as the other two
worlds, per person, and the record grows a section at a time. Up from the records in
`world/records.md`, through the counts, to what fires; down from the seller's questions; the cards
opened last.

## The bid and the champions: Gordon Ellery, Marta Olsen, Yvonne Pryce

Worked on 22 and 23 September 2026, with Colin Hurst, Alan Beck, Tessa Bright and Raj Kapadia
beside them, the four people named and never reached. Four cards: the clarification window at
Harrowgate, the price Marta asked for and the steps her side still has, and the introduction
Yvonne promised. Three of the four rest on reads no other world had run from the records up:
`deadline-they-gave`, `steps-on-their-side` with a filled process, and `they-owe-you`.

### 1. The records

Written in `world/records.md` as fiction, keeping every fact the cards implied where the facts
could all be true, and settling where they could not.

- **Fuller than either other world.** Eleven channels are connected, so a promise made on a call
  is on a transcript, a colleague's contact is on the CRM, and LinkedIn holds who is at each
  organisation. Gordon's date is in his mail; Yvonne's promise is in her mail and on the
  transcript of the day before; Marta's question is on a thread that turns in two days.
- **Your firm decided to bid on 3 September.** The interview said the seller must first convince
  her own company at an intake call, and no card said whether it had happened. The records put the
  intake on 3 September and Dale's line in the bid channel the same morning, "We're in", so the
  tender card's "Not Bidding" button is a change of mind rather than an open question, and F19 is
  a fact your firm records in its workspace, not a read.
- **Marta's question opened a promise of yours.** The card had her question of 24 August and
  nothing since; the records give your reply of the 25th, "let me put something together properly
  rather than guess", which is the acknowledgement that closes nothing and opens a promise, exactly
  as `their-question-unanswered`'s own rule says. The card counts both now.
- **Colin Hurst is a title.** LinkedIn says Chief Financial Officer; Marta has not named him, on
  any thread or transcript. The steps card holds his name back on that fact, as the council card
  held back Martin Hodgson's.
- **The consultation note exists.** The steps card offered "a short note on what comparable groups
  did about consultation" and the world's shelf had no such document; the buying process said you
  can partly help with that step "in a note". It is on the shelf now, and the card names it.
- **Alan Beck was on the wrong card.** Marta's People widget listed him, Stanmore's Head of IT,
  as Calderbank's missing IT lead. Calderbank's is a Group IT Director on LinkedIn whom nobody has
  named, and the row says so.
- **Stanmore published the plan Alan runs.** The annual report of April puts the asset register at
  the centre of a five-year digitisation programme. The card needed it, below.

Findings for the library, recorded in `coverage.md` and not fixed here:

- A read that forbids its own count as an opening (`they-owe-you`) writes no note on its own; it
  has to be paired with a read that supplies one, and the pairing is made by hand on the card.
- F19 is a record of the firm's own decision in the workspace, and nothing gathers a firm's
  decision about a deal; the deal seed carries the rung and that was enough here.
- Why a champion is invested (F5) is a told relationship note that neither champion in this world
  has, which is the thirty-fifth pass's "gathered by thirty-five reads and summed by none" met
  where it costs.

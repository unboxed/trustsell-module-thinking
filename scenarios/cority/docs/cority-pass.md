# The firm's world, worked from both ends

Job 3 of `NEXT.md`, run on the second world the library was not derived from: an enterprise seller
with a firm around her, seven cards, a patient tempo and a formal process on the buyer's side,
measured against the firm seller's twenty-one questions (`F`). The same six steps as the other two
worlds, per person, and the record grows a section at a time. Up from the records in
`world/records.md`, through the counts, to what fires; down from the seller's questions; the cards
opened last.

## What this world says, after the six steps

Seven cards were written for this world on 21 September to test the reads written for a seller
with a firm around her. Run from the records up two days later, all seven stand, two more exist,
and the ledger is this.

**Which cards should exist and do.** All seven, and none changed its move: the clarification
window, the price owed, the offer of help with their process, the introduction promised, the
cadence nobody mentioned, the name off the list, and the ninth touch. Two changed a read on the
way, `time-to-reconnect` off Ian's and `stated-priorities` onto Yvonne's, and both for the same
reason: the read that could open the note was the one that read what the buyer published.

**Which should exist and did not.** Two, written: the Ask about who has worked the conference
list, and the held question to Marta about who signs. One deferred on purpose: the brief, F14,
which Dale asked for on Friday at five past five and which is a floor above the cards. One that
should not be written, rightly: a note to Raj Kapadia, the IT door at Harrowgate, because the
tender's own rule is that questions go through the portal, and the record forbids it.

**Which should not exist.** None.

**What the message rows say here.** Every Act with a draft carries them, and this world added two
kinds of opening the council world never needed: the step their side has shown, for a note that
offers help with their process, and the buyer's own published plan on a card whose read forbids
opening on the promise it saw. The lesson is the same one four times now: a note opens on the one
thing the two of you already share, and the list of what that can be grows only when a card meets
a case the list had not.

**What no read says here**, in the order it would matter to the seller: what happened in the
last two weeks, in a paragraph (F14), which her manager has now asked for on record; why her
champion is invested (F5), which is a note she has never typed; the budget as a figure (F3), which
is on no record anywhere; who at the stand spoke to whom, which the list does not carry; and which
of the people she spoke to years ago is ready now (F21), which a read claims and cannot make
without a rhythm and a published reason, so it is answered by hand off the old thread. None of
these is a missing channel. This is the world where nothing is missing, and the holes are all
records the tool holds and does not read, or things only the seller could type.

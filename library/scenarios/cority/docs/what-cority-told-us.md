# What Cority told us: a patient sale with a firm around the seller

Read 21 September 2026 from the transcript of an interview on 17 December 2025 with an
enterprise account executive at Cority (a health, safety and sustainability platform), with the
user and two colleagues asking. Names are left out on purpose. The transcript is in the user's Downloads
(`2025.12.17 - Interview (Cority).md`). This note keeps what matters for the library. Everything
under *What she said* is hers; everything under *What it means for the library* is mine and not
yet confirmed.

There are two scenarios so far: BOPS, one seller selling patiently to councils, and PMF, a broker
moving one-owner deals in hours. Cority is not the middle between them. It is the patient end
again (18 to 24 months from first conversation to close, several people to win, champions), with
two things BOPS does not have: **a firm around the seller** (a business development rep who does
her outreach, solutions engineers who demo, marketing, a manager, a chief revenue officer, fifty
other reps whose calls she can listen to) and **a formal buying process on the other side**
(tenders, bids, procurement, hundreds of requirements). That is what a third scenario built on it
would test.

## What she said

**How it starts.** Both ways. Inbound, ideally before a tender is out, "so we have the time to
understand their business needs before we go into a procurement process". Outbound, which is where
big deals actually come from: she maps an organisation on LinkedIn Sales Navigator (posts,
interests, who they follow, buyer intent), reads their annual and sustainability reports for the
health and safety metrics, and builds a business case to reach out with. Two doors in: the business
side (a chief medical officer, a VP of safety, "people that will have visibility on strategy,
because we don't sell on features, we sell on why you want to change"), and IT (CTOs and CIOs
setting a digitisation strategy, who rarely decide but "know everything that's going on"). The
level she goes in at depends on the size of the account: "I'm not going to talk to a C-level at an oil major."
She asks IT how many applications they manage and what their budget is, which tells her what she
can sell and at what price. It is a small industry: people she spoke to five or six years ago with
no need come back "ready to have a conversation now".

**Her week.** About fifteen customer conversations a week, all at different stages. This week: four
first conversations ("we're interested, what do you do", probably six months before a real sales
process), and one in commercial negotiation and contract redlines, which takes the most time,
internally as much as externally.

**What wins.** "Any deal you win, you win very early, and it's because of how they engage with
you": they get people together when she asks, they put her in front of the stakeholders she has
named, they are discovering the best fit rather than running "a strict process on paper led by
procurement". Deciders rarely embed in the process; the work is finding who advises them and "who's
going to be talking on your behalf when you're not in the room", the champion. With one or two
champions "you're in a winning bid". A champion is invested for a reason (a career, a personal
reason, caring about the safety programme), and her job is to find that reason and make them look
good internally. No incentives to champions, ever.

**What she missed.** A prospect asked for pricing and she put it on the back burner "because it's
never about the pricing", for too long; they came back a week ago saying "we really need to have
the conversation". Her reading: if they were not chasing, they were not prioritising it either, but
she lost the chance to keep embedding herself as a trusted adviser. And after the first follow-up,
"what we lose a lot is a full process of: you haven't talked to this person in two weeks and there
was a deadline". A message on Teams saying "follow up on this" gets forgotten for a month. After a
conference, marketing hands her a spreadsheet of twenty-five people who attended and what they
were interested in, and the follow-up is hers, outside every system.

**Her firm.** She gives her business development rep the accounts, the personas and a talk track,
and he runs the outreach in SalesLoft: cadences (email, then a call two days later, then LinkedIn,
then another email), which she thinks "ends up being annoying"; "if they've not engaged after seven
reach-outs I don't think they have an interest, but this team will keep at it". The website's
contact form and "book a demo" go to that team, then to a person, then to her. Solutions engineers
demo, and now record two-minute videos so a "just show me the system" does not stall a deal. On a
bid she must first convince her own company to bid (an intake call), then bring a full team round
it; the bid team uses a repository of past responses and a tool that flags conflicts among hundreds
of requirements and suggests questions to ask back (the first output she got "made no sense").
Everything is recorded and integrated into Salesforce: anyone in the company can open her
opportunity and hear the calls. Gong records and transcribes calls, drafts the first follow-up
after a meeting, and lets her ask "what have I learned in the last week"; it learns from everyone's
conversations, so she can see how others run theirs. Forecasting is in Clari, which her VP presents
from: they see the stage and a small "next step" field, not the history, for fifty reps, so her CRO
still messages her at nine on a Friday asking where a deal is. What executives want: "a little
paragraph of latest activity, what's happened in the last two weeks".

**The process.** MEDDPICC in Salesforce (metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria and so on). Her
view: "there's a lot of fluff in these systems that nobody looks at"; the process she follows is
guided by the customer, "I sell for a living, you don't buy for a living, we are here to hand-hold
you through it". What no system does: "flag where the gaps are between where you need to be and
what the customer hasn't done. Have you had IT discussions? Have they got budget approved? Where
are the holes I need to help them fill?"

**Rules.** No regulation beyond GDPR: be able to say how you got someone's details, stop when they
ask. Formal ethics training twice a year, "the same at every company": no gifts. She is open about
how she got to someone ("I reached out to this person on LinkedIn that I knew, and they sent me
your details"). Her own incentives can cut across the buyer's timing: "they're telling me if you
bring it into Q4 we'll give you a 40% bonus", tracked in a separate system.

**On AI.** She uses none in selling except the call transcription and follow-up drafting, and
Copilot to turn a tender's language back at the customer in a presentation. Ranking leads by
likelihood to convert: "I don't think so." Suggested messages and timing: yes, "in two weeks, here's
an email, review it and schedule it", but "not fully automated, there still needs to be a very
personal side", because "it's so obvious to the person on the other side that you're going through
a checklist". Personal means "not just about my company but about the other company": feed it the
conversations, their documentation, their annual and sustainability reports, and have it suggest
what to write and which five priorities of theirs to raise. A tool that knew the whole context of
one customer across every channel "would probably save me days", one to two a week, on the digging
(their processes, their metrics, how she could show an improvement) and on the internal side (how
has this been done before, what did they save). What she would ask an assistant: "this is a
conversation I've had, this is the follow-up I want to do, this is their strategy: build me
something", an email or pointers for a presentation, case by case, and "really learning from a
two-way discussion, not standard responses you can tell are marketing".

## What it means for the library

*(My reading, not yet yours.)*

- **It is the scenario for "a firm beside you".** The seller's outreach is done by someone else at
  her firm, her calls are heard by fifty colleagues, and her manager and CRO watch the deal from a
  stage and a next-step field. V4 (a colleague already in touch) and the missing fourth word in
  `assumes:` have their natural home here, and so does `firm-contact`'s first real test: at Cority
  every contact by anyone is in the CRM, so the gather would be full rather than empty.
- **It confirms the patient end's reads in a second voice**, and Martyn's questions are not one
  man's: champions and what drives them (`champion`, and the told relationship note), the deal
  won early by how they engage (`warming`, `accepts-meetings`), the pricing request left too long
  (`loose-end`, and `pushing`'s "something they asked for" as the gift), seven unanswered
  reach-outs meaning no interest (`enough-tries`), the fortnight of silence with a deadline behind
  it (`cooling-champion`, `time-to-reconnect`), the contact from six years ago coming back
  (`time-to-reconnect`, `was-once-active`).
- **One read nobody has written: what the buyer has not done yet.** "Have they had the IT
  discussion? Have they got budget approved? Where are the holes I need to help them fill?" It is
  `missing-people` widened from who to what: the steps a buying decision needs on their side that
  the record shows no sign of. It needs a told list of what a buy at this kind of organisation
  requires (the shape of their process), which no told source holds. Neither question set asks it
  in those words; it is closest to Q11 and V7, and it is a third seller's question.
- **The seller's own urgency is a third shape of pushing.** A 40% bonus for closing a quarter
  early is pressure on the seller that the buyer's record does not back. `pushing` reads dates the
  draft would use against the buyer's words and your told pricing; it does not know about your
  incentives, and it should not need to: a date that is neither theirs nor a told price change is
  already manufactured. Worth saying in the read's body that the seller's own quarter is the
  commonest source.
- **The two-week paragraph for the boss is the floor above cards.** `library-format.md` defers
  "floors above the cards (insights, briefs)". Her executives' ask is the brief: what happened on
  this deal in the last two weeks, in a paragraph, from the same record the cards are built on.
  Noted so it is not re-invented.
- **Events are a lead source the channels do not have.** Twenty-five conference attendees with
  their interests, handed over by marketing as a spreadsheet, and a Teams message saying "follow
  up on this". They are leads (they came to you), so in scope, and nothing gathers them: the
  nearest is `web-form#form-submission`. A third scenario would need an "event attendee" record
  and Teams as a workspace channel beside Slack.
- **Public reports are what the buyer hands over before they hand anything over.** Annual and
  sustainability reports, safety metrics year on year, a change of strategy or executives: she
  reads them to build the case. That is `web#research-report` and `web#enrichment`, and it is the
  ground a first message stands on at this end. Later, procedures and hours are shared in
  conversation: `handover`, with no document.
- **Tenders are a document-set the buyer sends, in the other direction.** Hundreds of requirements
  to answer, an internal intake to win first, a repository of past answers. The library's
  `document-set` is what you asked for and what came; a bid is what they asked for and what you
  must send, and the fifth pass's rule (read what it states, nothing else) would apply to reading
  one. Out of scope for now, and the trade already has tools for it.
- **Her definition of personal is the tool's design.** The conversations plus their documents plus
  their reports, learned, and a draft to review and schedule, never sent for her. That is story
  chapters 3, 5 and 8 in one sentence from a seller who was not shown them. And her "I don't
  think so" to ranking leads by likelihood matches the scope decision: the tool covers from a lead
  onwards and does not choose among strangers.
- **Whether it earns a third scenario.** Not yet as a full world with cards: the map says a third
  earns its place once the two ends have shown what the spectrum is, and PMF's world is not
  written. What it earns now is a third question set. Her questions to herself are a
  different seller's (what has the buyer not done, who is talking for me when I'm not in the room,
  what did my own firm already do here, what happened in the last two weeks), and the coverage map
  would run them like the other two. That needs a doc with a "The list" heading and one line in
  `build.js` naming its filename and letter.

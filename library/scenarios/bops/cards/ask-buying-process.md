# What has to happen inside a council before anyone can sign?

I can see everything that passes between you and them. I can see nothing that happens in the room after you leave.

## Known so far

- The steps I have caught the shadow of, because somebody mentioned them: Holbrook's procurement window, which closes on 16 October. A committee at Felton that Stephen Walsh referred to in August. Nothing else.
- Two of them, out of a list I do not have. I do not know whether that is two of four or two of nine.

## What's missing

- The list itself. You have sold into councils for years and you know what it takes: a case for the spend, whatever assessment their rules require, a committee that has to sit, a signature. I have never been told any of it.
- What each one looks like when it has happened. A committee date is something they tell you. A business case is something you might never see. I need to know which is which, or I will treat your silence about a thing as their inaction on it.

## What your answer changes

- I hold every live council against your list and tell you which steps I have seen no sign of, and which of those you said you could help with.
- I can also tell you the shape of a sale: Westmoor ticking steps off without you means a process is running, and Felton talking for months with nothing started means they are still deciding what they want. Those are worked differently and today I cannot tell them apart.
- If you tell me you can supply the figures a case needs, that becomes an offer I can draft rather than a thing you remember to mention.

## Where your answer is saved

- As "what a buy at a council takes", against the kind of buyer rather than against one council. Editable, dated, and shown to you before it is ever used.
- I will never say a council has not done a step. I will say I have seen no sign of it and ask you. Work done in their building does not reach me, and the two look identical from here.

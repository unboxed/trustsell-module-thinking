# A list handed to you

The fourth place the tool learns from, beside what you connect, what you tell it and what the buyer
hands over. Somebody at your firm hands you names: the twenty-five people your marketing team
signed in at a stand, a campaign's responders, a territory a colleague worked before you, a list
the firm bought. None of it is yours, none of it is the buyer's, and nothing fetches it. It arrives
when a colleague sends it, usually as a spreadsheet.

Added 21 September, because the library had nowhere for a lead's **provenance** to live. Every
route a lead could arrive by was either a conversation the tool could see (a mail, a text, a form)
or invisible. A name on a list was invisible, so the one thing a seller most needs to be able to
say in a first message, where they got you and why that is not a cold call, was unsayable.
`source:` is `firm` for the same reason `handover` is `buyer`: nothing fetches it, and it is not
one of your accounts. *(My reason, not yet yours.)*

Whether it is plugged in is the scenario's to say (`connected:` in its `world/goal.md`), and a
seller with nobody around them never has one.

**What it carries is two things and not three.** The list, which says where it came from and who
handed it over, and the entries on it, one per person, with whatever the list holds about why they
are on it. It does not carry an opinion about whether a name is worth anything. Choosing among many
candidates is outside the tool's scope (the user, 20 September): the tool covers from a lead
onwards, so a list is a set of leads that have arrived, not a pool to rank.

**Whether you may say where you got them is a field, not an assumption.** A person who handed you
their badge at a stand expects to hear from you and can be told so. A bought list is usually the
opposite, and the difference matters enough that the list says which it is, and the reads above
will not put a provenance into a draft that the list has not cleared. *(My reason, not yet yours.)*

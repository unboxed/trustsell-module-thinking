# Selling to councils — one scenario, reverse-engineered

A worked walkthrough of a single real goal, run **backward**. Where `docs/flow.md`
walks the BOPS Plant goal forward in time, this one starts from a finished
recommendation and peels each layer back, asking *"what had to be true for this?"*
until we reach the raw data and the handful of things you told the tool once.

The point is to make the **build-up visible**: nothing here is allowed to float above
its raw material. Every insight is taken apart into the named facts it was reasoned
from. The example is illustrative — the exact names and numbers stand in for the real
ones; the *shape* is what matters.

**The goal, in the user's own words (condensed):** sell a new SaaS to local
authorities; a good product but a tough, multi-year sell. Find the early adopters —
individuals inside councils who feel the pain of legacy tech and want better tools for
their officers. Some can make buying decisions, some only influence them; either way
there are several stakeholders to win per organisation. There's a working shortlist of
about 30 organisations and named people, and the aim is to get them to commit to a
**partial** adoption now, with full adoption later.

---

## Layer 1 — The outcome (what you actually see)

One morning the tool surfaces a single card:

> **Act on Gary Pearson at Council 14 today.** He's quietly become your strongest
> advocate there, and he's just asked a question that opens a door — whether the
> product can import their existing case records — and he's pulled his head of IT into
> the thread. Lead with the records-import answer and the East Marston case study, and
> offer the partial rollout as the easy first step. Here's a draft in your voice.

That's the whole output: who to act on, why now, what to include, and a ready message.
Everything below is what it took to produce that one card.

**The card, taken apart into its ingredients:**
- *who to act on* — Gary, surfaced by Relationships
- *why now* — the live question and the warming pattern, Relationships' reading
- *what to lead with* — the records-import answer and the partial-rollout first step
  (Offerings), and the East Marston case study (Organisation)
- *the words themselves* — rendered in your voice (Profile), inside the compliance fence
  (Organisation)
- *the fact it was chosen today, over everything else* — Brain's triage

So even the finished card is an assembly of pieces from five modules. Keep pulling.

## Layer 2 — The decision (why this, out of a hundred quiet things)

At any moment you have about 30 organisations and several people in each, mostly
inching along. On a normal day almost nothing is urgent. So Brain's real job here isn't
to write — it's to **choose**: of everything quietly in motion, where did something
just shift in a way that rewards a move *now*?

Brain weighed Gary against the rest of the board, and his situation won on a few counts:
- **something genuinely changed** — a new question and a new stakeholder appeared,
  while most of the board is static today
- **it fits the goal's shape** — a champion widening the coalition inside a target org
  is exactly the kind of progress this goal wants
- **it's gently time-sensitive** — an unanswered question from an engaged person goes
  stale if ignored, and these relationships are too valuable to leave hanging
- **it's low-risk to act** — answering a question he asked is welcome, not pushy, which
  matters because the strategy says move patiently

This is a judgement Brain makes fresh each time, weighing the whole board against the
goal. It is not a standing routine — it's the **decision in the middle** that only
Brain can make, because only Brain sees everything at once and holds the goal. (The
module docs call this a *play*.)

Notice the patient tempo doing real work: a more aggressive strategy might have
surfaced three moves this morning; this one surfaces the single move that has a
natural, welcome reason to reach out, and lets the rest keep simmering.

## Layer 3 — The pieces, and how they compounded into the brief

Before Brain could decide, it gathered a piece from each expert. Each piece is itself a
small reading, and the brief is what you get when you combine them:

- **Relationships** handed up: *"Gary is a warming champion with a live question,"* plus
  the loose end (his records-import question is still unanswered) and the stakeholder
  picture at Council 14.
- **Offerings** handed up: the answer to a records-import worry, the partial-rollout
  first step that's easy to say yes to, and the proof that reassures a cautious public
  buyer.
- **Organisation** handed up: the East Marston County case study (a comparable council
  already live) and the compliance fence the message must stay inside.
- **Profile** handed up: the voice the message is written in, and the preference to see it
  before it sends.

Brain compounded those into the **brief**: *act on Gary, because he's your advocate and
he's opening a technical door; lead with the records answer and the East Marston story;
offer the partial rollout; keep it warm, not salesy.* Profile then rendered the brief into
the actual words, and Organisation did a final compliance pass.

So the brief is a **compounded** thing: its ingredients are other modules' readings,
not raw facts directly. To reach the raw data, we have to open up the biggest
ingredient — Relationships' reading.

## Layer 4 — The reading, taken all the way apart (the centrepiece)

*"Gary is a warming champion with a live question"* is really **three smaller readings
stacked together**, and each one rests on specific raw facts.

**"Gary is a champion"** — read from a behaviour pattern, every line a plain fact
fetched from your mail and calendar:
- he started four of the last six email threads himself, rather than only replying —
  *from the senders and timestamps in your mail*
- his replies land within a day and run several paragraphs — *from the gaps between
  messages and their length*
- he writes with ownership: *"once we've rolled this out," "I'll put it to procurement"*
  — *from the words in his messages*
- he's copied two colleagues into threads over time — *from the changing list of
  participants*
- he set up and attended two internal meetings about the product — *from your calendar*

None of that is a tag you applied. Together, the facts **are** the verdict "champion."

**"He's warming"** — read not from the facts themselves but from how they're *trending*:
- his replies have been getting faster over the last few weeks
- the circle keeps widening — last Tuesday he added his head of IT, a name on no
  earlier thread
- he's been in touch more often lately

**"There's a live question"** — read from one specific fact:
- his most recent message asks whether the product can import their existing case
  records, and it's still unanswered

And underneath all of it, **two things you told the tool once** — the only user input in
the whole stack — which are what make the insight *worth raising* rather than merely
true:
- Council 14 is one of your 30 target organisations
- your goal is to land a partial adoption and grow it, so an unanswered implementation
  question from a champion who's pulling in technical people reads as a door opening,
  not as trivia

Strip the facts away and there is no insight; strip your two inputs away and the insight
is real but pointless.

**The stakeholder map at Council 14**, decomposed the same way. The unit of work here
isn't a lone decision-maker — it's a small coalition, and the tool builds a picture of
it:
- **Gary (service-design lead) — champion.** Read from the behaviour above.
- **Rachel (head of IT) — newly involved, stance unknown.** Read from her first
  appearance on Tuesday's thread; nothing yet tells the tool whether she's an ally or a
  sceptic, so it holds the question open rather than guessing.
- **A budget holder — known to exist, not yet seen.** Read from the gap: nobody on the
  threads so far has the authority to buy, and the goal needs someone who does, so the
  tool flags the *absence* as the next thing to find.

The map is the same kind of assembly as the reading: roles inferred from who appears,
how they behave, and who they're copied alongside — with the holes called out as
honestly as the knowns.

(Here the deal and the coalition coincide, because everyone in this effort sits inside
Council 14. In general the **deal** is the unit, and it can pull in people across
organisations; this single-org coalition is just its simplest shape. See
[`02-relationships/assemblies/deal.md`](../assemblies/deal.md).)

## Layer 5 — The routines that produced the reading

How did Relationships have this reading ready? Because it runs a standing watch. In
plain terms, its routine is:

> Every morning I sweep the mail, calendar and Slack for the people across the 30
> target organisations, and I keep a running read on each one. In between sweeps, the
> moment something moves on one of them — a reply lands, a new name appears on a thread,
> a meeting is made or missed, a silence stretches too long — I look at whether it
> changes my read and whether it matters to the goal. When it does, and it points at a
> move, I raise it.

The other modules run lighter routines:
- **Offerings** keeps the council pitch sharp and ready — the entry step, the proof, the
  objection answers — and doesn't go hunting; it answers when asked.
- **Organisation** keeps the case study and the compliance fence current.
- **Profile** stands ready to render in voice.
- **Connections** has no routine at all. It only moves when another module reaches
  through it to fetch or to send.

Restating the distinction in this scenario: the watches above are **routines** — each
has one owner and a standing job. The priority call back in Layer 2 is a **play** — no
single owner, a decision in the middle. Both are needed; they're just different shapes.

## Layer 6 — The strategy the routines came from, and what Brain needed first

The routines didn't appear from nowhere; Brain wrote them from the strategy. And the
strategy didn't appear either — Brain built it *with you*, out of your paragraph. That
synthesis is the first real act in the whole system, so it's worth slowing down on.

**What Brain drew out of your paragraph:**
- the offering, and a two-step finish line: partial adoption now, full adoption later —
  so every org sits somewhere on that ladder
- the tempo: tough, multi-year, low urgency — which is itself an instruction to nurture
  patiently and never push, because a clumsy chase costs a relationship built over years
- the target, which is a *mindset* — people who feel the legacy-tech pain — not a
  category like "councils"
- the buying structure: several stakeholders per org, deciders and influencers both, so
  you win a coalition rather than a contact
- the starting board: about 30 named orgs and people, already in flight

**What Brain needed before it could synthesise** (it isn't instant):
- a goal shaped well enough — Brain may come back with a few questions to fill the gaps
  ("what counts as 'engaged'?", "what's the easy first yes?", "who are the 30?")
- the modules' starting material — the pitch loaded into Offerings, the accounts
  connected so there's data to watch at all, your seed list of orgs and people
- a first baseline scan — a pass over the whole board so it knows where each
  relationship currently stands, before it can say what's worth doing first

Only once those are in place can Brain turn "sell to councils" into concrete standing
work — *"Relationships, watch these 30 for these movements; Offerings, sharpen these
answers"* — and the machine starts to tick.

One twist this goal puts on the three kinds of work: because the shortlist of *orgs* is fixed,
**Plant** here mostly isn't new logos — it's planting new *people*, widening the coalition inside an
org you already have (find Gary's colleagues and win them too). Planting counts whether the ground is
new or one you already hold; *moving the deal forward* with the people already in it is Grow. So
Plant turns inward, while nurture and grow carry most of the weight.

## Layer 7 — The raw-material floor

Keep peeling, and everything bottoms out in two piles, and only two.

**Fetched** — pulled in through Connections, the only thing that reaches outside:
- the back-and-forth in your mail: who wrote, when, how fast, how long, to whom, and
  what was said
- the meetings on your calendar: what was booked, kept, moved, or dropped
- mentions in Slack: who's talking about whom in the deal channels
- what the open web says: a council's new digital strategy, a leadership change, a
  tender, a public talk

These arrive as plain facts with no meaning attached. Connections never reads them.

**Told** — what you, and only you, put in. It's a short list:
- the goal itself
- the seed list of which orgs and people to chase
- your voice, and your preferences (such as "show me everything before it sends")

And the line it took us a few tries to get right: **characterisation is not in the told
pile.** You don't tell the tool that Gary is a champion. The tool reads that off his
behaviour — and that reading is the entire point of the Relationship module. You supply
the goal, the targets, and your voice; the tool supplies the *meaning*. Strip the
fetched facts and there are no insights; strip your few inputs and the insights are real
but aimless. Put both piles together and you get a card that says: act on Gary, today,
because — and here's the draft.

## The shape of it

Read from the bottom up, that's the picture: many raw facts at the wide base, narrowing
through readings into a few insights, compounding into one brief, plated into one action
at the point. Not a route you travel — an **assembly you build**. `docs/flow.md` shows
the same machine moving forward in time; this shows it standing still and taken apart,
so you can see what every layer is made of.

This assembly is now a live view in the blueprint app — the **Assembly** tab
(`/assembly`), the canvas's complement to the route trace. It builds the picture from
the bottom up: the raw facts appear first, then the grey-to-blue "reading" line, then
the readings, the insight, the gathered pieces, and finally the action glowing at the
apex. (The words there are mirrored from this doc — keep the two in sync.)

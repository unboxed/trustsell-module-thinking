# Their words, or somebody else's
## What it counts

Each passage sorted into two piles: published by the organisation itself, on a document or a domain
that is theirs, or written about them by somebody else. A search tool's synthesised answer is
always the second pile, however well it reads, because it is the tool's sentence and not theirs.
Which records it needs and when the number stops meaning anything are not written in the count's
fields yet, like every other count.

It counts within [`published-picture`](../assemblies/published-picture.md), decided when the count
was written rather than derived.

## What it cannot see

A press release a buyer wrote that a trade publication printed under its own byline, which is their
words in somebody else's pile. And a consultant's report the organisation commissioned and
published, which is the reverse. The sorting is by who published, not by who wrote, because who
published is a fact and who wrote is usually not. That is the safer error to make: it under-claims
what can be quoted back rather than over-claiming it.

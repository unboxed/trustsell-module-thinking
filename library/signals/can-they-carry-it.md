# Whether they can carry it

## What it means

Whether this buyer can bear what you are selling on top of what they already carry. It is the read
that can say no to a sale that would close.

[`fit-match`](fit-match.md) asks whether a lead is the kind of buyer the offering suits.
[`price-barrier`](price-barrier.md) asks whether your price will come up. This asks the question
after both: even if they fit and even if they will pay, should they. A council whose budget cannot
take it this year and a merchant who cannot carry a second repayment are the same read.

It is made per buyer, from what they have handed over or said, never from a guess about their
kind.

## In practice

A lead has sent six months of statements and the form you asked for. What you sell would cost
them a fixed sum a month over a year. What they have handed over shows what comes in each month
and what already goes out to two other commitments. The read: "on what they have shown you, this
would take more of each month than is left after what they already owe. Do not put this to them as
it stands; a smaller amount or a longer term might carry, or nothing might."

## How it is worked out

Applies the shared reading rules in [`docs/reading-principles.md`](../docs/reading-principles.md).
Borrows People's deal for what the buyer has said on the thread.

- Trigger: what the offering would cost them over its term (counting, from your told price) is set
  against what they can bear (counting, from what they handed over or stated) net of what they
  already owe (counting, from the same). The read lands when the first exceeds the second. There is
  no fixed share of income that means "cannot carry": the line is whatever the buyer's own figures
  show, and the one judgement is how much room to leave under it. That judgement is not written
  yet, and the read says so rather than picking a number.
- Confidence: high when the cost is a told fact, the term is known and the buyer's figures come from
  a document they handed over; softens to "may not carry" when what they can bear rests on a figure
  they said in passing rather than showed.
- Needs: what the buyer hands over, for what they can bear and what they already owe. Without it
  the read rests only on what they said on a thread or your form, and softens accordingly (a data
  gap, named not faked). What the tool reads inside a document is written since 21 September
  (`channels/handover.md`: the figures a document states, and nothing else), and two reads beside
  this one describe it, [`uneven-income`](uneven-income.md) and
  [`existing-commitments`](existing-commitments.md). The term and the way it is paid are told
  fields on the offering's pricing since 21 September; where they are empty for this offering the
  cost over the term cannot be summed (a told gap, named not faked), and the read says so.
- Assumes: nothing about the shape of the sale. It is made per buyer from their own figures, so it
  stands on a lead of a fortnight as well as on a customer of years.
- This read points against a sale. It sits in Offerings and not in Organisation on purpose:
  Organisation is optional and a solo seller can skip it, and refusing to oversell someone who
  cannot bear it must hold for a solo seller too.

# What they say matters to them

## What it means

What this organisation has said, in its own published words, that it is trying to do.

A buyer's plan, strategy or annual report is the one place they have written down what matters to
them without you in the room. It is not a guess about their motives and it is not what their
industry generally wants. It is a passage, on a page, with a date on it.

The read names those passages, says how fresh each one is, and says which are the organisation's
own words and which are somebody else's about them. It stops there. What to do with one is the
move, and the move is not this read's.

## In practice

An organisation published a four-year plan in September. Page 12 commits it to replacing its legacy
case systems by 2028. Its site has a page on the same subject last touched in 2021, and a trade
publication wrote about the plan the week it came out. The read: "they have put one thing in
writing that your offering ends up at, on page 12 of a plan published this month, in their own
words. The page on their site saying the same thing is five years old. The trade piece is not
theirs to be quoted back."

## How it is worked out

Applies the shared reading rules in [`docs/reading-principles.md`](../docs/reading-principles.md).

- Trigger: the organisation has published something that states an intention, and it is recent
  enough to still be theirs (counting). A priority with no date is held as undated rather than
  assumed current.
- What it says: the passage, where it is, when it was published, and whose words it is. The passage
  matters more than the summary, because a line you can quote is the whole value of this read and a
  paraphrase of it is worth nothing in a first message.
- Confidence: high where the words are the organisation's own, dated, and on a document they
  published. Softens where the words are a search tool's synthesis or a third party writing about
  them, and softens again where the only date is the day the page was crawled. A five-year-old page
  is not a priority, and the read grades on age rather than dropping it, because an old commitment
  a buyer never withdrew is still worth knowing about.
- Needs: the open web, which is builtin and always on. The gap here is not a disconnected channel,
  it is an organisation that publishes little, which is most private companies and few public
  bodies. Where there is nothing, the read is silent. It does not fall back on what the sector
  generally says it wants, because a sector's priorities in a buyer's mouth is exactly the
  manufactured relevance [`pushing`](pushing.md) exists to stop.
- Assumes: nothing about the shape of the sale. It stands before a conversation exists, which is
  the point of it: this is the only read in the library that can be made on an organisation you
  have never contacted.
- It answers F1 ("what does this organisation say about itself in public that I could build a case
  on"). It does not decide which of those priorities your offering should speak to. That is
  Offerings' [`lead-with-this`](lead-with-this.md), which reads this gather for the buyer's half
  and its own told use-cases for yours. Splitting it this way keeps the buyer's words in the module
  that owns the buyer, and the matching where the catalogue is. *(My reason, not yet yours.)*

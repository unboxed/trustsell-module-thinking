# Warmth fading

The softest read in the library, and labelled as such. Where Cooling and Warming count *whether*
someone engages (speed, gaps, who starts), this asks the murkier question of *how* they come across: is
the warmth still there, or has it gone flat? It is the read for the case where the replies still arrive
on time but something in them has cooled. It earns a place only as a quiet flag that sits on top of a
firmer read, never as a verdict on its own.

| Floor | What People has |
|---|---|
| **Facts** (1) | the messages with Anna; her earlier warm, chatty replies against her recent ones; the words and notes from the last two captured calls |
| **Counts** (2) | *warmth in replies:* down from ~70 words with a greeting and "thanks so much!" to ~10 words, no greeting · *friendly words:* warm, enthusiastic words tail off across the period · *talk-time on calls:* on the last call she spoke about a third as much as the call before |
| **Opinion** (3) | **"Anna may be going cooler in how she comes across"**: still replying, but shorter, flatter, and quieter on the call · *confidence: low* |

What surfaces is offered as a prompt, not a finding: *"Anna's gone flatter, shorter replies, less
warmth, and she said much less on the last call, worth a look alongside whatever else you're seeing."*
The countable clauses walk back to message lengths and talk-time; the warmth reading does not fully,
and the read says so.

**Threshold.** A change measured against the person's *own* earlier warmth, never an absolute: a
naturally short writer is not "going cold" for being short, and the read only leans on a clear move
away from their own usual length, friendliness and how much they say on a call. Because the underlying
judgement is soft, the bar to even raise it is higher than for the counting reads, and it is held at
**low** confidence on purpose.

**Why it's only partly trustworthy, and says so.** This is the honest exception to "most of the read is
plain counting". The hard parts (how reply length is trending, how much of a call they spoke) are real
arithmetic. But reading *warmth* from words is genuinely arguable: tone over text is unreliable, and
sarcasm, culture, mood and shorthand all fool it. So the read never fires alone or acts on its own. It
is a low-confidence flag whose job is to add colour to a firmer signal (a Cooling champion, a Blocker),
and it is always shown with its evidence so a person can glance and wave it off in a second.

**Where it can fail to reach ground.** The text half misreads anyone whose style is naturally clipped.
The call half exists only when note-taking was on: a call nobody captured leaves no record to hear tone
in, the conditional-content **data gap** [`meeting-history`](../assemblies/meeting-history.md) names.
With neither half on solid ground, the read steps back to "nothing reliable to say" rather than
inventing a mood.

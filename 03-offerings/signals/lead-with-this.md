# Lead with this

A read that points the angle: for this kind of buyer, which use-case to lead with, which information
is most relevant, and what to hold back. It feeds the Brain's message play (the "match the message to
the person" group, Q10–12 and Q24): Offerings supplies the angle, the Brain decides the move.

| Floor | What Offerings has |
|---|---|
| **Facts** (1) | the told use-cases on file for BOPS (full migration · partial rollout you grow · pilot); the fit-shape factors a public buyer cares about (continuity · risk · audit); the worries on the objection list |
| **Counts** (2) | *use-cases on file:* 3 · *dominant worry for this buyer-type:* rollout risk and data residency top the list for public buyers · *relevance to stated factors:* partial-rollout maps to continuity and risk (2 of the 3 factors they lead with), full-migration maps to none of them |
| **Opinion** (3) | **"Lead with the partial-rollout use-case"**: it speaks to the continuity and risk a cautious council leads with, and sidesteps the rollout-risk worry; hold full-migration for later · *confidence: graded* |

What surfaces is not "send the deck" but *"for a cautious council, open on partial-rollout: it speaks
to the continuity and risk they care about, and it sidesteps the rollout-risk worry that tops their
objection list."* Every clause walks back to a told use-case and a worry on the list.

**Threshold.** There is no fixed "always lead with X". The angle is chosen against *this buyer-type's*
own concerns: which of the factors they care about a use-case actually speaks to, and which dominant
worry it sidesteps or invites. A use-case that maps to none of their stated factors is not the opener,
however strong it is in general. The read needs at least one use-case on file and a fit-shape that
names what the buyer cares about; with neither it has nothing to rank.

**Why it's trustworthy.** Most of the read is deferred counting: how many use-cases are on file, which
worry tops the list for this buyer-type, how many stated factors each use-case maps to. The one
judgement, which angle leads, is guarded by ranking against the told factors rather than taste. The
`confidence` is **graded on grounding**: high when use-cases and fit-shape are richly told, soft when
only one use-case is on file or the buyer-type's factors are thin.

**Where it can fail to reach ground.** *Which* use-case actually *wins* (not just maps best) is the
parked learning loop: until outcomes are wired, the read ranks by relevance, not by track record.
That is named, not faked. And if you have told it no use-cases, there is nothing to lead with, a
**told gap**.
